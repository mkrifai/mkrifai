### 🌐 About & Research Anchor

I am a physicist and disaster-risk researcher interested in understanding how environmental hazards translate into spatially differentiated risks for people, infrastructure, and landscapes.

My research journey began in geophysics and climate science, and has increasingly moved toward disaster risk, remote sensing, and geospatial analysis. I work with Earth and environmental data to investigate hazards and their spatial patterns, with particular interests in climate-related risks, flood and drought, land and environmental change, and disaster impacts in Indonesia.

My approach combines physical understanding with quantitative and spatial methods. Depending on the problem, I work across remote sensing, GIS, spatial analysis, environmental and climate modelling, and geophysical methods. I am particularly interested in questions where a technically accurate model or map is only the beginning:
- *What does the pattern mean?*
- *Who is exposed?*
- *Why does risk differ between places?*
- *And how can scientific evidence become useful for real-world decisions?*

Beyond research, I am developing an interest in science–policy translation and public-facing scientific communication.
